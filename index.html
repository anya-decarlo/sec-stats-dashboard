Descriptive statistics

Why a table: precise values beat pictures for summary numbers. Three windows — annual, quarterly, YoY growth — because each answers a different question (level, seasonality, volatility).

Trend & fit

Why a line chart: ordered time on the x-axis — the line encodes continuity. Fitted curves are overlaid, not replacing the points: the model should never hide the data. Toggle log scale when growth is multiplicative.

Residual check

Why bars of residuals: a model isn't validated by its R² — it's validated by where it's wrong. Residual spikes locate the years the trend story breaks.

Growth & outliers

Why bars with flag coloring: growth is a change measure — bars anchored at zero keep sign readable. IQR-fence and z>2 outliers are colored for investigation; click one to open its case file.

Growth distribution

Why histogram + box plot: bars answer "which years?"; distributions answer "what's normal?" The box plot puts the entities' spreads side by side — shape comparison is what it's for.

Revenue → income relationship

Why a scatter: two continuous variables, one question — does top-line growth translate to bottom-line? Regression line + R² quantify it; a line chart would wrongly imply time order.
